Who the plan is for

A family can be parents and small children, grandparents, or adults who still want the trip to feel like time together. Write who is going, and the ages that change the day. A nap, a late dinner, or a day with no schedule belongs in that note.

Pace, and what to skip

The useful plan names the first night, the long days, and the ride home. It also names what you do not want. That can be a park every day, a long transfer, or a dinner that runs past bedtime. We shape those choices with you after you write. We do not pick a pace and hand it over as finished.

What to send

Send the group, the kind of trip, and the dates you have in mind. Dates can be a month or a season. A destination can be a city, a coast, or somewhere warm. The form on the home page is the place to send that.

A checklist before you write

  • Who is going, and any age that changes the day
  • The month or the dates you can travel
  • How full you want each day to be
  • What you want to skip
  • Whether this trip is the whole vacation or one piece of a longer one

If you are deciding what to write

Can the dates be rough?

Yes. A month, a season, or a week you hope to leave is enough to start.

Do you need every name on the first note?

No. Who is going, in the way you would say it out loud, is enough. Ages help when a child changes the plan.

Where does the request go?

The form on the home page stores the request and starts the conversation. This page is the guide. It is not a second form.
